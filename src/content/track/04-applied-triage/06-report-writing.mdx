<Why asked="Show me an example of your written work.">
Analysis that nobody can follow has the same value as no analysis. Your tickets
are the durable output of your shift, the thing Tier 2 acts on, the thing a
post-incident review reads six months later, and the thing that forms your
reputation before anyone has met you. It is also a graded component of BTL1, and
it is the artifact most likely to be requested at interview - which makes it the
highest-leverage soft skill in this entire track and the one candidates most
consistently neglect.
</Why>

## Write for the person who was not there

Every reader is missing the context you have right now. Assume they know the
platform but not this alert, and that they are reading under time pressure. Three
audiences, three different documents:

| Audience | Wants | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 2 / next shift** | What you did, what you found, what is unresolved | Ticket notes |
| **Incident manager** | Impact, scope, confidence, recommended action | Escalation summary |
| **Business / executive** | What happened, what it means, what is being done | Executive summary |

## BLUF - bottom line up front

Lead with the conclusion. Not the chronology of your investigation, not what fired
first - the answer.

**Weak:** *"I received an alert at 09:14 and began by checking the sign-in logs.
I then looked at the device compliance status, and after that I reviewed..."*

**Strong:** *"Confirmed account compromise of p.osei via token replay, with
subsequent access to 847 HR-confidential files and 3.8 GB exfiltrated. Containment
recommended immediately. Detail below."*

The reader can now decide how urgently to read the rest. That is the entire
purpose.

## The escalation template

The one you will use most. Six sections, in this order:

```text
1. SUMMARY        One or two sentences. The conclusion and the ask.
2. SEVERITY       Your rating, with the reasoning in one line.
3. TIMELINE       Normalised to UTC. Timestamp, source, what happened.
4. EVIDENCE       What you checked and what it showed. Queries, event
                  IDs, artifact names. Enough for someone to verify.
5. ASSESSMENT     What you conclude, your confidence, and what would
                  change it.
6. RECOMMENDATION Specific actions, in priority order. Say what is
                  within your authority and what is not.
```

<Callout type="key">
Section 5 is the one that separates good analysts. **State your confidence and
what would change it.** "High confidence - the combination of Tor-exit source,
unregistered device and post-sign-in file access has no benign explanation I can
construct. This would change if the user confirms travelling with a new device,
which I have asked IT to verify." That sentence tells the reader exactly how much
weight to put on your conclusion, which is worth more than false certainty.
</Callout>

## Language discipline

**Distinguish observation from inference.** "The process opened a handle to
lsass.exe with access mask 0x1010" is an observation. "The attacker dumped
credentials" is an inference. Both belong in the report - clearly labelled as what
they are. Blurring them is how wrong conclusions get repeated as fact by people
three steps removed from the evidence.

**Use calibrated confidence language** and use it consistently:

- *Confirmed* - directly observed in the evidence
- *High confidence* - strong evidence, no reasonable alternative explanation
- *Moderate confidence* - evidence supports it, alternatives exist
- *Low confidence / possible* - consistent with, but far from established
- *Unable to determine* - a legitimate and respectable finding

**Avoid hedging that means nothing.** "It appears that there may possibly have
been some suspicious activity" says less than "unable to determine whether the
file was executed; no process creation event was recorded."

**Write plainly.** No "in today's evolving threat landscape". No "leveraged" where
"used" works. The reader is busy.

<Callout type="warn">
Never write "closed, false positive" or "closed, benign" without evidence. The
next analyst cannot verify it, the tuning team cannot act on it, and if the
decision was wrong nobody can reconstruct why. A closure needs the same rigour as
an escalation, just less of it - what you checked, what it showed, why it is
benign.
</Callout>

<div slot="model">

```text
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ESCALATION - INC-2026-0714-003
Account compromise with confirmed data exfiltration
Analyst: [name] · Raised: 14 Jul 2026 11:20 UTC
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. SUMMARY
Confirmed compromise of user account p.osei via probable token
replay, following a successful password spray from a Tor exit
node. The attacker accessed 847 files on an HR-confidential
share, compromised the svc_backup service account, established
service persistence, and exfiltrated 3.8 GB to an external
host. Immediate containment is recommended. Personal data is
likely in scope.

2. SEVERITY: CRITICAL
Confirmed exfiltration of HR-confidential material, two
compromised identities including a privileged service account,
and active persistence at time of writing.

3. TIMELINE (all times UTC; FILE-SRV-02 logs converted from BST)

09:12:04  Password spray begins, 185.220.x.x (Tor exit).
          34 accounts, one attempt each, all status 50126.
09:14:41  p.osei signs in successfully from 185.220.x.x.
          MFA satisfied. Device UNREGISTERED.
09:22:09  Network logon (4624 type 3) to FILE-SRV-02 from
          LAPTOP-P-OSEI (10.20.9.14).
09:22:11  4672 - special privileges assigned to session.
09:24:53  5145 - 847 files read from \\FILE-SRV-02\
          HR-Confidential.
09:31:18  p.osei signs in from 92.40.x.x on compliant device
          LAPTOP-P-OSEI. Assessed as the legitimate user.
09:41:02  svc_backup authenticates from LAPTOP-P-OSEI.
          Expected source is BACKUP-01 only.
09:41:30  7045 - service "WinTelemetrySvc" created,
          ImagePath C:\Windows\Temp\wts.exe.
09:47:55  Outbound beaconing begins to 185.220.x.x:443.
          62 connections at ~60s intervals, ~1.2 KB each.
10:52:14  Single outbound flow, 3.8 GB, to 45.61.x.x:443.

4. EVIDENCE CHECKED
- Entra sign-in logs: spray pattern (one attempt per account
  across 34 accounts), success for p.osei, device registration
  state and MFA method on both sign-ins.
- FILE-SRV-02 Security log: 4624 type 3, 4672, 5145 share
  access with file count.
- Perimeter firewall: connection records for 10.20.9.14,
  interval and byte counts.
- Asset inventory: 10.20.9.14 confirmed as LAPTOP-P-OSEI;
  svc_backup expected source confirmed as BACKUP-01.

5. ASSESSMENT
High confidence this is a genuine compromise, not a false
positive. The combination of a Tor-exit source, an
unregistered device satisfying MFA, and privileged file
access seventeen minutes before the legitimate user signed
in from home has no benign explanation I can construct.

MFA satisfaction from an unregistered device indicates token
replay rather than credential-only compromise (T1550.001).
Password reset alone will therefore NOT terminate access.

svc_backup authenticating from a user laptop is assessed as
credential theft rather than misconfiguration, given the
timing relative to the file access.

Confidence would reduce if: the 09:14 sign-in is explained by
a corporate VPN egressing via that IP range (assessed
unlikely - Tor exit), or if svc_backup is legitimately used
from endpoints (needs confirmation from the platform team).

TECHNIQUES: T1110.003, T1078.004, T1550.001, T1021.002,
T1039, T1543.003, T1071.001, T1041

6. RECOMMENDATIONS (priority order)
a. Isolate LAPTOP-P-OSEI, preserving memory. Do not power
   off - the beacon process and any unpacked payload are in
   volatile memory.
b. Revoke all sessions and refresh tokens for p.osei.
   A password reset alone is insufficient.
c. Identify all systems using svc_backup BEFORE disabling,
   then rotate. Disabling blind will break backups.
d. Block 185.220.x.x and 45.61.x.x at the perimeter.
e. Determine the contents of the 847 files - this drives
   any notification obligation and the clock has started.
f. Review Entra audit log for OAuth consent grants, inbox
   rules and MFA method changes on p.osei.

OUTSTANDING - not yet completed by me:
- Which of the 34 sprayed accounts are valid, and did any
  other succeed
- Estate-wide hunt for the beacon interval and both IPs
- Whether svc_backup authenticated to any other host

Actions a-d are outside Tier 1 authority and are raised as
recommendations.
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

**What makes this work, and what to copy into your own template:**

- **The summary answers the question before any detail.** A manager reading only
  section 1 knows the severity, the impact and the ask.
- **The timezone conversion is stated explicitly** in the timeline header. Anyone
  re-checking your work knows what you did.
- **Evidence is separated from assessment.** Section 4 is what the logs say;
  section 5 is what you think it means. A reader can disagree with your conclusion
  while still trusting your facts.
- **Confidence is calibrated and falsifiable.** You said what would change your
  mind and who needs to confirm it.
- **The recommendations are specific and ordered**, and two of them carry a warning
  about *how* to execute - don't power off, don't disable svc_backup blind. That
  operational awareness is what earns trust.
- **The outstanding section is honest.** Listing what you have not done is not an
  admission of failure; it prevents Tier 2 assuming coverage that does not exist,
  and that assumption is how things get missed during handover.
