<Why asked="Why SC-200 rather than another certification?">
This is the credential that changes your CV's reception the most for the money.
UK SOC job specs overwhelmingly name Sentinel and Defender, and SC-200 is the
vendor's own attestation that you can operate both. Combined with CEH, it reads
as offensive knowledge plus defensive tooling - which is precisely the profile an
MSSP hiring a career-changer wants to see. It is also the only paid item in this
track besides BTL1, so it needs to earn its place, and it does.
</Why>

## Before you spend anything

<Callout type="warn">
**Check the official skills-measured document before you start revising.** Exam
objectives are revised periodically and the weightings shift. Anything you read
elsewhere - including this page - may describe an older version. Download the
current PDF from the exam page, and note the "skills measured as of" date at the
top. Studying last year's objectives is the most avoidable way to fail.
</Callout>

**Hunt for a voucher before booking at full price.** Historically the routes worth
checking have included Microsoft Virtual Training Days (free sessions that have
often come with a discounted or free exam voucher), Microsoft Learn Cloud Skills
Challenges, and periodic Microsoft certification promotions. Availability changes
constantly, so check current offers rather than assuming - but check, because the
difference is most of the £165.

**Book the exam before you feel ready.** Set the date roughly three weeks out. An
open-ended plan to sit it "when I'm confident" reliably becomes never, and the
booked date is what converts study into revision.

## What the exam covers

Broadly, three domains - confirm the current split and weightings against the
official document:

**Mitigate threats using Microsoft Defender XDR** - the four pillars, incident
management, investigation and response actions, custom detections, and the unified
portal. Everything in the previous step.

**Mitigate threats using Microsoft Defender for Cloud** - the one area this track
has not covered directly. Secure score, regulatory compliance, workload
protections, and connecting cloud resources. **Budget extra time here** - it is
the most common gap for people arriving from a pure SOC-analyst background.

**Mitigate threats using Microsoft Sentinel** - workspace design, connectors and
DCRs, analytics rules, entity mapping, KQL, hunting, workbooks, automation and
incident management. Steps 1 to 3 of this phase.

## Question formats to expect

- **Multiple choice and multiple response** - the bulk of it.
- **Case studies** - a scenario with several linked questions. Read the scenario
  once fully before answering anything.
- **Drag and drop / ordering** - sequence the correct configuration steps.
- **Hot area** - select the correct option in a rendered interface.
- **Yes/No series** - several statements against one scenario. These are graded
  independently and **cannot be revisited once you move on**, so answer carefully
  the first time.

Passing is 700 out of 1000, which is a scaled score rather than 70 percent of
questions.

## A three-week revision plan

**Week 1 - close the known gap.** Work the entire Defender for Cloud learning path
on Microsoft Learn. This is the domain your practical work has not touched, so it
is where cramming actually helps. Do the hands-on exercises rather than reading
past them.

**Week 2 - objective sweep.** Take the skills-measured list line by line. For each
bullet, rate yourself confident / shaky / unknown. Study only the shaky and
unknown. This is far more efficient than re-reading material you already own, and
it is how you avoid spending week two revising KQL you can already write.

**Week 3 - practice and mechanics.** Take the free official practice assessment
repeatedly until you are comfortably over the threshold. Review every wrong answer
until you understand *why* the right one is right, not just which it was. Then
stop studying the day before and rest.

<Callout type="key">
**The practice assessment is free and official.** Microsoft publishes one on the
exam page. Do not pay for third-party question dumps - they are frequently
outdated, often against the certification agreement, and they teach you a specific
question set rather than the material. Your labs, Microsoft Learn and the official
practice assessment are sufficient.
</Callout>

## Exam-day mechanics

- **Online or test centre.** Online proctored requires a clear desk, a clear room,
  a room scan and no interruptions. If your home environment is not reliably quiet
  and private, a test centre is worth the trip.
- **Run the system check in advance** if you are sitting it online. Do this days
  before, not on the morning.
- **Flag and move on.** Do not lose eight minutes on one question. Flag it, keep
  the pace, return at the end - except the Yes/No series, which you cannot revisit.
- **Read the constraint in each question.** Many are decided by a phrase like
  "minimises administrative effort" or "at the lowest cost" - two answers work,
  only one satisfies the constraint. This is the single most common way people lose
  marks on Microsoft exams.
- **You get provisional results immediately.** The formal transcript follows.

## If it does not go your way

The retake policy allows a resit after a short waiting period, with a longer wait
after subsequent attempts, and the resit is chargeable. You get a score report
showing performance by domain - use it, because it tells you precisely where to
work. A failed first attempt is a common and recoverable event, not a verdict on
whether you belong in the field.
