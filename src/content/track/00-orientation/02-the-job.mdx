## A realistic shift

A Tier 1 analyst in a UK MSSP works a rota — commonly four on, four off, days
and nights. A night shift looks roughly like this:

**Handover (20 min).** The outgoing analyst walks you through open incidents,
anything escalated to Tier 2, and any customer with a change window running that
will generate noise. You take notes. This is a genuine skill and you already
have it from the shop floor.

**The queue (most of the shift).** Alerts arrive from a SIEM. You triage each
one: what fired, why, is it real, and if it's real how bad. Most are not real.
A brute-force alert that's a service account with a stale password. An impossible-travel
alert that's a VPN. A malware detection that's a security team running their own test.
You close them with evidence, and you escalate the handful that survive scrutiny.

**Escalation (a few times a shift).** Something is real, or you can't rule it
out. You write it up — what fired, what you checked, what you found, what you
recommend — and hand it to Tier 2. The quality of that write-up is most of what
your reputation is built on.

**Quiet hours.** At 4am on a Sunday the queue can go dead. Good analysts use it
to hunt, learn, or write up documentation. This is where the people who progress
separate from the people who stay Tier 1 for four years.

<Callout type="warn">
Be honest with yourself about nights. You said you'd do shifts for a while to get
in, which is the right call — but a four-on-four-off rota with nights is genuinely
hard on sleep, relationships and health. Plan an exit: most people move to Tier 2
or a day-shift role in 18–24 months. Go in with that timeline explicit rather than
discovering at month nine that you have no plan.
</Callout>

## The UK market shape

**MSSPs** are your highest-probability entry: Bridewell, Quorum Cyber, e2e-assure,
Adarma, Talion, Integrity360, SecurityHQ, Bulletproof, Phoenix, Socura. They run
24/7 rotas, hire in volume, and are the most willing to take a certified
career-changer with lab evidence. Expect roughly £28–35k regionally, £35–48k in
London for Tier 1.

**In-house SOCs** in finance, retail and healthcare pay similarly or better but
hire fewer juniors and usually want a year of something first.

**Defence and government** — Leidos, CGI, BAE, QinetiQ, plus GCHQ-adjacent work
around Cheltenham — sponsor SC and DV clearance and are unusually open to
non-IT backgrounds. Long stable employment in manufacturing is exactly the
profile vetting likes. Slow process, worth starting early.

<Callout type="key">
Clearance is an asset you can start building before you're qualified. If you're
willing to go through SC vetting, say so on every application — it removes cost
and delay for the employer and genuinely differentiates you.
</Callout>

## Pull apart five real job specs

This is the actual work of this step, and it's worth the hour.

Go to a job board, find five UK Tier 1 SOC adverts, and for each one build a
three-column table: **required**, **desirable**, **what they actually mean**.
That third column is where the learning is. "Familiarity with SIEM platforms"
means *can you write a query without hand-holding*. "Strong communication skills"
means *your tickets have to be readable by someone who wasn't there*. "Analytical
mindset" means *you don't close things you haven't checked*.

Then count. How many mention Microsoft Sentinel or Defender? In the UK it will
be most of them, and that is precisely why Phase 3 is weighted so heavily.
