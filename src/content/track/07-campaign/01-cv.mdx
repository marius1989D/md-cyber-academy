<Why asked="Talk me through your CV.">
Your CV has one job: get you into a conversation. It is read twice - once by an
automated filter looking for keywords, and once by a human giving it under ten
seconds before deciding. A conventional chronological CV fails you badly here,
because the most recent thing on it is manufacturing and the most relevant thing
is buried at the bottom. Restructuring is not spin; it is putting the relevant
evidence where a reader will actually see it.
</Why>

## The structure for a career changer

Standard order buries you. Use this instead:

```text
1. Name, location, contact, LinkedIn, GitHub/portfolio       2 lines
2. Professional summary                                       3-4 lines
3. Certifications                                             short list
4. Technical projects            <- does the heavy lifting    ~40% of page 1
5. Technical skills                                           grouped
6. Professional experience       <- translated, not hidden
7. Education
```

<Callout type="key">
**Projects sit above work history deliberately.** A reader scanning for relevance
finds it in the top third instead of the bottom of page two. This is a recognised
format for career changers, not a trick - and if asked about it, "I led with the
security work because that is what the role is about" is a perfectly good answer.
</Callout>

**Two pages. UK conventions:** no photograph, no date of birth, no marital status,
no full address - town and county is enough. Do not include a "References
available on request" line; it wastes space on something universally assumed.

## The professional summary

Three or four lines, factual and specific. Not a personality statement.

**Weak:** *"Passionate and highly motivated cyber security enthusiast seeking to
leverage my skills in a challenging SOC environment where I can grow."*

Every applicant writes this. It says nothing checkable.

**Strong:** *"CEH v13 and BTL1-certified security analyst with a purpose-built
detection lab covering Microsoft Sentinel and Defender XDR. Wrote and validated 12
detections across 8 ATT&CK techniques, tested against 31 Atomic Red Team
implementations. Background in high-volume manufacturing process monitoring and
fault diagnosis under 24/7 shift operations. SC clearance eligible."*

Every clause is verifiable, and the last two lines pre-empt the two questions a
recruiter has about you.

## Translating manufacturing experience

This is the part to get right, and the principle is: **describe the transferable
mechanism accurately, do not claim security work you did not do.**

| What you did | How to write it |
|---|---|
| Monitored process parameters against spec | Monitored continuous process telemetry against established baselines, identifying deviations before they caused defects |
| Responded to machine alarms | Triaged automated alarms under time pressure, distinguishing genuine faults from nuisance trips to avoid unnecessary line stoppages |
| Diagnosed defects (short shot, flash, sink) | Performed systematic root-cause analysis on production faults, isolating variables across a multi-factor process |
| Escalated to process engineers | Escalated unresolved faults with documented diagnostic steps taken and findings, enabling rapid handover |
| Followed setup sheets and SOPs | Executed documented procedures precisely in a regulated environment; contributed to procedure updates |
| Shift handover logs | Maintained written handover records across a 24/7 rota, ensuring continuity between shifts |
| ISO 9001 / IATF 16949 documentation | Maintained audit-grade documentation to automotive quality standards, subject to external audit |

<Callout type="warn">
**Do not write "monitored for security threats" or anything implying you did
security work on a production line.** It is untrue, it will not survive a follow-up
question, and it destroys your credibility on everything else in the document. The
transferable claim is about *the shape of the work* - baseline monitoring, alarm
triage, diagnosis, documented escalation - and that claim is both true and
genuinely relevant.
</Callout>

## Writing project entries

Each project gets a title, one line of context, and three or four bullets. Every
bullet needs a verb, a specific technology, and where possible a number.

**Weak:** *"Built a home lab with Sentinel and wrote detection rules."*

**Strong:**

> **MD AI Cyber Range** - self-built detection engineering lab
> - Deployed Microsoft Sentinel with Azure Monitor Agent collecting Windows
>   Security and Sysmon telemetry from segmented lab hosts, with ingestion
>   capped and cost-alerted from day one
> - Simulated 10 ATT&CK techniques using Atomic Red Team, documenting the
>   telemetry each produced and identifying 3 collection blind spots
> - Wrote 12 KQL detections validated against 31 atomic implementations;
>   reduced false positives on the highest-volume rule from 42/day to 2/day
>   through scoped parent-process conditions
> - Published detections in Sigma format for cross-platform portability;
>   submitted one rule upstream to SigmaHQ

Four bullets, eleven numbers, no adjectives.

## Surviving the ATS

Applicant tracking systems match keywords from the job advert. This is mechanical,
not clever:

- **Mirror the advert's exact terminology.** If it says "SIEM" write SIEM, even if
  you wrote "Sentinel" elsewhere. If it says "Microsoft Sentinel" use the full name
  at least once.
- **Spell out and abbreviate on first use** - "Endpoint Detection and Response
  (EDR)".
- **Avoid tables, columns, headers, footers and text boxes** for the machine-read
  version - many parsers mangle them. Keep a clean single-column version for
  applications and a designed version for direct human contact.
- **Submit as PDF unless the advert says otherwise**, and name the file
  `Marius-Dumitru-CV-SOC-Analyst.pdf` rather than `CV_final_v3.pdf`.

<div slot="brief">

A first attempt. Diagnose everything wrong with it and specify the fixes.

```text
MARIUS DUMITRU
[full home address, postcode, DOB, photo]
Milton Keynes | 07xxx xxxxxx | email

PERSONAL STATEMENT
Highly motivated and passionate individual looking to break into the
exciting world of cyber security. I am a fast learner with excellent
communication skills and a keen eye for detail. I am seeking a
challenging role where I can utilise my skills and grow within a
dynamic organisation.

EMPLOYMENT HISTORY
Injection Moulding Technician | [Company] | 2018 - present
  - Operated injection moulding machines
  - Responsible for quality checks
  - Worked as part of a team
  - Health and safety compliance

Warehouse Operative | [Company] | 2016 - 2018
  - Picking and packing
  - Forklift operation

SKILLS
  Microsoft Office, Teamwork, Communication, Problem solving,
  Time management, Windows, Networking, Hacking, Cyber security

CERTIFICATIONS
  CEH v13
  Currently studying CompTIA SecAI+

INTERESTS
  I enjoy building computers, reading about technology, and
  keeping up with the latest cyber security news.

References available on request.
```

</div>
<div slot="model">

**The single biggest problem: thirty weeks of security work appears nowhere.**
There is no projects section, no lab, no detections, no portfolio link, no BTL1.
Everything that qualifies him is missing, and everything present is either
irrelevant or actively working against him. Nothing else matters until that is
fixed.

**Working through it:**

**Header.** Remove the photograph, date of birth and full address - UK convention,
and they invite unconscious bias while wasting space. Town and county only. **Add
LinkedIn and GitHub URLs**, which are currently absent and are two of the most
valuable lines available.

**Personal statement.** Every clause is unverifiable and every applicant writes
it. "Passionate", "fast learner", "keen eye for detail", "dynamic organisation" -
none can be checked and none differentiate. Replace with the factual summary
format: certifications, what was built with numbers, the transferable background,
clearance eligibility.

**Missing: certifications should move up.** They currently sit below skills and
above interests. CEH and BTL1 are among the strongest signals on the document and
belong on the top third of page one.

**Missing entirely: a technical projects section.** This is the fix that changes
the CV's outcome. The lab, the detections, the Sigma contribution, the write-ups -
all of it, with numbers, positioned above employment history.

**Employment history.** The bullets describe duties rather than capabilities, and
none is translated. "Operated injection moulding machines" tells a security
recruiter nothing. "Responsible for quality checks" understates systematic fault
diagnosis. Rewrite using the translation table - baseline monitoring, alarm
triage, root-cause analysis, documented escalation, audit-grade records under a
24/7 rota. **Keep the warehouse role but reduce it to a single line** - it is real
work history and gaps invite questions, but it needs no detail.

**Skills.** Several problems. "Microsoft Office" is assumed and wastes a line. The
soft skills - teamwork, communication, time management - are unverifiable filler
that every CV lists. **"Hacking" must go**; it is imprecise and reads as
unprofessional to a hiring manager. "Cyber security" as a skill is meaningless on
a cyber security CV. Replace the whole section with grouped, specific technologies:

> **SIEM & Detection:** Microsoft Sentinel, KQL, Defender XDR, Sigma, Splunk (SPL)
> **Endpoint & Forensics:** Sysmon, Autoruns, Volatility, KAPE, Eric Zimmerman tools
> **Frameworks:** MITRE ATT&CK, NIST 800-61, Pyramid of Pain
> **Platforms:** Windows, Linux, Entra ID, Azure, VMware, OpenWrt

**Interests.** Delete. "Building computers and reading about technology" is
weaker than the projects section that should replace it, and it reads as filler
where evidence should be.

**"References available on request."** Delete. Universally assumed, and it costs a
line.

**One thing done right:** the certifications are listed accurately, with SecAI+
correctly marked as in progress rather than claimed. Keep that honesty - claiming
an unheld certification is the fastest way to be removed from a process.

**The redrafted structure:** contact with links, factual summary, certifications,
technical projects with numbers, grouped technical skills, translated employment
history, education. Two pages, single column, no tables.

</div>
